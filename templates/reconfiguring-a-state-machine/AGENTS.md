## Reconfiguring a State Machine Note

This track should feel more industrial, more provisional, and a little rougher than the others.

The paper should feel:
- implementation-oriented
- mechanically grounded
- like a working control surface rather than a polished lecture
- comfortable admitting where the problem gets ugly

Use the existing visual language as the anchor:
- clipped panels and control-plate framing
- cyan, amber, and green signals on dark steel neutrals
- diagrammatic icons that feel operational
- a sense of instrumentation rather than decoration

Keep the reading experience practical:
- prioritize interfaces, boundaries, and operational constraints
- make reconfiguration feel like a live systems problem
- use callouts to mark real hazards, not just side notes
- let roughness exist where the subject is actually rough

Avoid turning this paper into:
- a clean minimal product site
- a generic dark-mode technical blog
- a playful or whimsical experience
- a heavily polished surface that hides the complexity

The goal is for this track to feel like you are getting closer to the machine room: less myth, more control logic.
