Section 1.3 Assumptions

The requirements of the parliamentary protocol could be achieved only by providing the legislators with the necessary resources. Each legislator received a sturdy ledger in which to record the decrees, a pen, and a supply of indelible ink. Legislators might forget what they had been doing if they left the Chamber,3 so they would write notes in the back of the ledgers to remind themselves of important parliamentary tasks. An entry in the list of decrees was never changed, but notes could be crossed out. Achieving the progress condition required that legislators be able to measure the passage of time, so they were given simple hourglass timers

If a legislator is a node, what is a decree? What is a ledger. The ledger is some way of it being recorded that stable. It could be memory, it could be a database, it could be json document. That part isn't important to Lamport. He is more interested in the process of how they are agreed to and recorded. He also is making concrete the requirement that each node owns it's own recording and that it isn't a shared between them. So a database they were all using as their main ledger would not count not would it truly be distributed then, that would be centralized.

If the requirements are consensus between nodes these are the tools he's giving

Any of the other metadata, the "notes in the margins" cannot be counted on to always be there and accessible. Messages, IE messengers might never be delivered or delayed. Time must be kept locally at least in a simplistic manner, IE the hourglass timers. And there must be a retry mechanism, what those are are outside the main scope of the paper.

One assumption he is not concerned with is disagreements among the legislators. He is assuming they all trust each other, no Byzantine faults, no malicious actors, and no clients trying to propose different values for the same decrees. He is basically saying transactions are not an problem this is to address and is outside the scope.
