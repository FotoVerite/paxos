Introduction

The authors are putting forth that while Paxos is a robust way of dealing with failure prone systems there's a disconnect with how it's being used in the wider world. Paxos is mostly being used for configuration management, not actual data replication, which is where real systems diverge from theory

The authors including Lamport state that the abstract models (which he defined himself) did not fully capture the requirements of real systems. In particular, the original formulation assumes a fixed replica set, whereas real systems require continuous reconfiguration and system-wide resilience. I would argue that even here the focus remains largely on the mathematical model, and a significant amount of additional work is required to translate these ideas into real systems with real code.

For the authors Vertical Paxos provides mathematical foundations for existing primary-backup replication protocols found in the wild, allowing those systems to be analyzed and their tradeoffs made explicit rather than treated as ad-hoc.

Vertical Paxos also allows reconfiguration to happen concurrently with requests being processed by the system, avoiding the usual tradeoff between reduced fault tolerance and service disruption during state transfer.
