Optimization

Phase 2 is all you need
Since failure of the leader and election of a new one should be rare events, the effective cost of executing a state machine command—that is, of achieving consensus on the command/value—is the cost of executing only phase 2 of the consensus algorithm. It can be shown that phase 2 of the Paxos consensus algorithm has the minimum possible cost of any algorithm for reaching agreement in the presence of faults. Hence, the Paxos algorithm is essentially optimal.
LAMPORT, P. 11 — §3

Okay, so he's heavily referencing a Part Time Parliament here. If one assumes failures of leadership is rare one can batch Phase 1 for all future proposals. Meaning one can use the same proposal numbers continually and just go through Phase 2 cutting down massively on chatter and message passing.

Without out that knowledge this seems absolutely unitelligatibale and unprovable. Much like the rest of the paper he's so stuck up his own ass he doesn't care about actually explaining to the reader what the fuck he actually is driving at.
