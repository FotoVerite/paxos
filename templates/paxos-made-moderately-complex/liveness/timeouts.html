Timeouts & Ballots

Weak leader election relies on timeouts. The idea is simple: if a leader isn't heard from (isn't making progress), another leader should try to take over.

How timeouts drive progress

Each ballot has an associated timeout. When a leader claims a ballot and runs scouts, it starts a timer. If the leader gets a majority to adopt the ballot, great—it uses that ballot to propose values.

But if the scout times out (doesn't get a majority in time), the leader gives up on that ballot. It picks a higher ballot and tries again. This new ballot has a longer timeout (to avoid spinning too fast).

This is the mechanism that prevents livelock: failing ballots are abandoned, and higher ballots are tried. Eventually, a ballot will succeed and stay stable.

Timeout choices

Picking timeouts is tricky. Too short, and you'll abandon good ballots due to temporary network delays. Too long, and you'll wait forever when a leader has actually crashed.

Van Renesse suggests multiplying the timeout by a factor (like 2) each time a ballot fails. You keep increasing the timeout until eventually it stabilizes. And with each successful ballot (each decision), you can decrease the base timeout to start from a lower value.

This is a heuristic, not a guarantee. But in practice, it works well.

Safety is never at risk

Here's the important part: changing ballots and timeouts doesn't affect safety. Safety comes from the ballot ordering and acceptor rules, not from timing.

So Paxos can be run in any timing model: synchronous, asynchronous, or partially synchronous. The only thing that changes is liveness. In a synchronous system (where you know message delays), you can guarantee that timeouts will eventually work. In an asynchronous system, you can only guarantee eventual progress, not bounded progress.

The ballot numbers

In practice, ballot numbers are crafted to encode both round number and leader ID. For example, a node might use ballots like (0, nodeID), (1, nodeID), (2, nodeID), etc. This ensures that if node A and node B are both trying to be leader, their ballot numbers automatically order them.

The protocol doesn't care how you encode ballots, only that they have a total order.
