Live-Lock

Definition

Consider the following scenario, with two leaders with identifiers $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$ such that $\lambda < \lambda'$. Both start at the same time, respectively proposing commands $c$ and $c'$ for slot number 1. Suppose there are three acceptors, $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, and $\alpha_3$. In ballot (0, $\lambda$), leader $\lambda$ is successful in getting $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ to adopt the ballot, and $\alpha_1$ to accept pvalue $((0, \lambda), 1, c)$.

Now leader $\lambda'$ gets $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_3$ to adopt ballot $(0, \lambda')$ (which is after $(0, \lambda$) because $\lambda < \lambda'$). Note that neither $\alpha_2$ nor $\alpha_3$ accepted any pvalues, so leader $\lambda'$ is free to select any proposal. Leader $\lambda'$ then gets $\alpha_3$ to accept $((0, \lambda1), 1, c')$.

RENESSE, P. 1 §1

Liveness refers to the system's ability to make progress. Given a client request, it should eventually receive a response. If it does not, the system can be said to be in a failure state, due to either a live-lock, deadlock, or other failures.
