Scout Execution Model

Scout Thread
The leader must enforce Invariants C1 and C2. Because there is only one leader per ballot, invariant C1 is trivial to enforce by the leader not spawning more than one commander per ballot number and slot number. In order to enforce Invariant C2, the leader runs what is often called a view change protocol for some ballot before spawning commanders for that ballot. The leader spawns a scout thread to run the view change protocol for some ballot b. A leader starts at most one of these for any ballot b, and only for its own ballots.
RENESSE, P. 6 §2 - Scouts, Passive and Active Modes

Whenever a Leader increments its ballot number it must ensure that a quorum of acceptors recognize it as the greatest ballot they have seen. The view change here is a change in the leader’s internal state from inactive to active for that ballot. It does this through a scout. This scout sends $(p1a, \lambda, b)$ messages to all known acceptors, asking them to respond with $(p1b, \alpha, b, r_\alpha)$.

Scout Thread
A scout completes successfully when it has collected $(p1b, \alpha, b, r_\alpha)$ messages from all acceptors in a majority (again, guaranteed to complete eventually), and returns an $(adopted, b, \bigcup_{r_\alpha})$ message to its leader $\lambda$.
As we will see later, the leader uses $\bigcup_{r_\alpha}$, the union of all pvalues accepted by this majority of acceptors, in order to enforce Invariant C2.
RENESSE, P. 6 §2 - Scouts, Passive and Active Modes

This states that if the scout receives $(p1b, \alpha, b, r_\alpha)$ messages from a quorum of acceptors, it reports success to the leader by sending an $(adopted, b, \bigcup_{r_\alpha})$ message. The leader then enters the active state for ballot $b$, and the scout exits. $r_\alpha$ here refers to all pvalues accepted by acceptor $\alpha$.
