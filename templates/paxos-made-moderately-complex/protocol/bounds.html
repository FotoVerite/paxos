Protocol Bounds

Definition

n a purely asynchronous environment it is impossible to determine through pinging or any other method whether a particular leader has crashed or is simply slow. However, under fairly weak assumptions about timing, we can design a version of Paxos that is guaranteed to choose a proposal. In particular, we will assume that both the following are bounded

the clock drift of a process, that is, the rate of its clock is within some factor of the rate of realtime;

the time between when a non-faulty process initiates sending a message, and the message having been received and handled by a non-faulty destination process.

We do not need to assume that we know what those bounds are—only that such bounds exists.

RENESSE, P. 9 §3 - When Paxos Works

While failure detection with a 100% certainty is impossible. Messages might simply be delayed. By setting a upper bound to these delays we can with a high decree of certainty assume a failure has occurred.

By assuming that clock-drift is small, IE, that a millisecond in a system is reasonably close to an actual millisecond and that messages are processed in a timely fashion we can make assumptions on reasonable bounds to how long to wait.
