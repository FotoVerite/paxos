Ballot

Definition
In the Synod protocol, there is an infinite collection of ballots. Ballots are not created; they just are. As we shall see later, ballots are the key to liveness in Paxos.
A ballot has a unique identifier ... called its ballot number. Ballot numbers are totally ordered, that is, for any two different ballot numbers, one is before or after the other. Do not confuse ballot numbers and slot numbers; they are orthogonal concepts. One ballot can be used to decide multiple slots, and one slot may be considered by multiple ballots.
RENESSE, P. 4 §2

Ballots form an unbounded, totally ordered set. When Renesse says ballots “are not created,” he means that the protocol reasons over a pre-existing infinite ordering, not over ballot creation events.

Ballots contain an identifier called a ballot_number. Implementations may represent ballots as integers or tuples. In the protocol, ballots are used to order competing proposals so replicas can determine which proposals win.

For the papers purpose a ballot identifier will be represented as (r, λ). r refers to the monotonically increasing round number. λ refers to the Leader Identifier.

A ballot is not tied to a single command, client, or slot; the same ballot may be used while proposing values for multiple slots. A ballot number of 10 can be used in a slot numbered 8 and vice versa.
