Real Implementations

Paxos isn't just theory. It's deployed in some of the most critical systems in the world.

Google Chubby

Chubby is Google's lock service. It uses Paxos to replicate lock state and configuration data. Chubby has been running for many years and powers much of Google's infrastructure.

Key insight: Chubby doesn't use all the optimizations. It prioritizes simplicity and correctness over raw throughput. This makes it a reliable building block for other systems.

Apache ZooKeeper

ZooKeeper is the open-source equivalent of Chubby. It's used in Hadoop, Kafka, and many other distributed systems.

ZooKeeper implements Paxos-like consensus (it's called Zab, a variant optimized for totally ordered delivery). It provides a file-like interface for distributed coordination.

etcd and Raft

etcd uses Raft, another consensus algorithm. Raft is simpler than Paxos to understand and implement, though it's less efficient in some scenarios.

etcd powers Kubernetes and is widely used for service discovery and coordination.

Amazon DynamoDB and other databases

Many modern databases use Paxos or Paxos-like algorithms for replication. DynamoDB uses it for high availability across data centers.

Why Paxos (or variants)?

These systems use Paxos or algorithms inspired by it because:

  • Proven correctness: Paxos has been formally analyzed. You know it won't lose data or violate consistency.
  • Asynchrony: It works in asynchronous networks with unbounded delays.
  • Fault tolerance: It tolerates minorities of failures.
  • Practical: With optimizations, it's fast enough for real systems.
